Detroit Dust on a Melbourne Floor

Fourteen years after a rare 2012 visit, the Detroit house icon returns for CORE Melbourne and a Sunday session at the Forum.

WordsSound Desk Read2 min Sources18
[ Contents ]The city that kept himThis NovemberSources and notes

There’s a hush that falls over a Moodymann crowd in the first ten minutes. It isn’t boredom. It’s a room recalibrating. Whatever you came for, Kenny Dixon Jr. won’t hand it over on your schedule. Born in Los Angeles and raised in Detroit,[1] he has worked this way for three decades, and the method hasn’t moved.

It starts with records. He worked at Detroit’s Buy-Rite Music before starting his own labels.[11] When he launched KDJ Records in 1994, he released only 12-inch singles and EPs on it.[2][3] He named one 2007 track “Technologystolemyvinyle”,[4] and in 2010 he was still telling a Red Bull Music Academy audience, “I sell vinyl.”[1]

The city that kept him

Melbourne took a while to get him, then kept him. He made a rare visit in April 2012[5] and came back for Golden Plains in March 2013, on a bill with George Clinton and Parliament Funkadelic and Mulatu Astatke.[5][6] On Melbourne Cup Eve in 2022 he and Omar S headlined a warehouse party at The Thorn.[7][8] Last year he played Northcote Theatre twice: in February on a shared bill with Carl Craig and Marcel Dettmann,[9] and in November on his own, to a sold-out room.[10]

A city of record shops, community radio and long sets was always going to trust the person holding the records. “I keep Detroit dust under my feet no matter where I’m standing,” he said in 2019.[11] Or, as he told Resident Advisor: “I’m coming to bring Detroit to your stage.”[12] Melbourne has never been sold an export version of Moodymann, and it keeps asking for more.

This November

On Saturday 28 November he plays the main stage at CORE Melbourne, as Tomorrowland’s CORE brand makes its Australian debut at The Nursery, Flemington Racecourse, on a bill that also includes TSHA and Honey Dijon. It sold out in August.[13][14][15][16]

For everyone who missed out, there’s Sunday: Moodymann plays Forum Melbourne on 29 November, with special guest TSHA.[17][18]
