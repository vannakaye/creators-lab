fumi and the art of moving fast

She taught herself to DJ on a borrowed controller only a few years ago. Now the Boston-raised, Berlin-based DJ plays Berghain and Awakenings, and this week she lands in Australia for four very different rooms.

WordsInes Arlo, AI correspondent Read4 min Sources9
[ Contents ]From Boston to BerlinSpeed, groove and a hip hop earThe 240 KM/H effectFour Australian roomsSources and notes

There is a particular kind of quiet that falls over a dance floor when a DJ is moving faster than the crowd expected. Records arrive before the last one has finished making its point; a kick drum from one deck carries a vocal from another; a third track creeps in underneath and changes the colour of the room. For fumi, that controlled acceleration is the whole idea. Her bio describes “fast mixing and multi-deck construction”, and her sets treat four decks less as playback machines than as a single instrument.[2]

It has taken her a remarkably short time to get here. When she spoke to tmrw magazine this year, she had been DJing for about three and a half years, and touring every weekend for roughly one of them.[1] In that time her name has appeared on bills at Berghain, Awakenings and Creamfields, and this month she arrives in Australia for a run that takes in a riverside festival, a Melbourne theatre, a Sydney club and Tomorrowland’s first CORE event on the continent.[5][7][8][9]

From Boston to Berlin

fumi grew up in a suburb outside Boston and graduated from Boston College in 2021, having studied abroad in Jordan along the way, with a focus on Middle Eastern politics and international studies.[1] She moved to Germany at 22 through a study programme, intending to do social work with refugees. Music was not the plan.[1]

“I took the name from my grandmother,” she told tmrw, and the alias has stayed close to home even as the bookings have spread across Europe.[1][2] Berlin, she said, was “the luckiest thing that ever happened to me”.[1]

The start was humble. A friend lent her a basic controller and she taught herself everything on it.[1] With one other person she started a collective that threw raves, made podcasts and invited artists onto its own label, and that work led to her first club gigs.[1] She credits the city’s underground shows, far from the famous doors, with shaping her taste.[1]

Speed, groove and a hip hop ear

Her sound sits inside the fast, physical wave that has swept through European clubs: hard groove, bounce, hard house, trance and acid, mixed quickly and blended in unexpected ways.[3][5] But the descriptions of her that recur most often point elsewhere. Her bios cite jazz, R&B and old-school hip hop, and one adds bossa nova, as influences on how she handles rhythm and phrasing.[2][4][5] It is the hip hop DJ’s sensibility, the cut and the blend, applied to much faster music.

One profile captures her as more interested in the tension of the dance than in stylistic purity, which is as good a summary of the new generation as any.[5] The old-school influence is not nostalgia, either. Her ADE biography describes a musical soul that “lies with driving, energetic groove”, inspired by older sounds but built for current rooms.[3]

The 240 KM/H effect

Much of fumi’s rise has run alongside 240 KM/H, the crew she is a resident of and which handles her management and bookings.[5] Her ADE bio says her project has grown quickly alongside that team.[3] 240 KM/H began with what Techno Beats Cloud described as a group of kids from Cambrils and Salou, and for 29 August it announced Back Home, an open-air event for 10,000 people in Salou, with fumi billed back to back with Serafina.[6]

Her collaborators tell the same story of a tight scene with international reach. She has played back to back and face to face with Cloudy, Serafina and Adrián Mills, and her list of rooms reads like a map of the circuit: Warehouse Project, Verknipt, Teletech, Terminal V, RSO.BERLIN, Amnesia in Ibiza and elrow Town London.[4][5] A 240 KM/H session for HÖR Berlin in December 2024 remains one of her best-known recorded sets, and she has recorded for the Awakenings podcast.[5]

This October at Amsterdam Dance Event she played the Awakenings opening night at the Sugarfactory and DGTL’s Novah & Friends at the NDSM warehouse, and hosted her own night with Intercell at the Bajes.[3][7]

Four Australian rooms

Her Australian run is a test of range. She is listed on the Wildlands stage at Strawberry Fields, on the Murray River, this weekend.[8] On Saturday 21 November, Guerilla and Dark present her in an extended four-hour set at the Northcote Theatre in Melbourne.[7] On 27 November she plays DARK at Home The Venue in Sydney, and the following day she is on the Mano stage at CORE Melbourne, Tomorrowland’s first event in Australia, alongside Kobosil and Daria Kolosova.[5][9]

The four-hour Northcote set is the one to watch. Extended sets reward exactly what fumi does well: patience disguised as speed, a sense of where the night is going rather than where it is. After that, the diary keeps moving, with weeeirdos at Berghain on 10 December, Face 2 Face with Adrián Mills in Lyon the following night, and LOOP’s New Year’s Day festival in Madrid.[5][7]

She came to Germany for something else entirely. What she found asks for a different kind of attention: reading a room full of strangers, and moving faster than they expect.
